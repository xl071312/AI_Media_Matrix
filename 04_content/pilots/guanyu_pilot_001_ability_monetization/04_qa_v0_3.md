## Cognitive Gain Nodes

1. **Ability is supply; buyer value is consequence.** Skill labels describe the seller, not the buyer's changed outcome.
2. **A problem becomes commercially relevant when it carries continuing cost.** Cost may be money, time, opportunity or certainty.
3. **Capability needs result translation.** A buyer needs to understand what changes and whether that change can be checked.
4. **Payer, decision authority and trust are separate gates.** Solving a problem is not enough if the seller cannot reach the budget-holder or reduce first-transaction risk.
5. **One-off success is not yet a monetization system.** Repeatability requires explicit checklists, indicators, templates, process and review loops.

### MODEL / INFERENCE
- Buyers generally evaluate a capability through expected consequences rather than the capability label itself.
- A continuing costly problem is more monetizable than a vague inconvenience, all else equal.
- Describable/checkable/comparable results reduce ambiguity in a transaction; they do not guarantee purchase.
- A smaller first transaction can reduce decision risk; it does not guarantee conversion or repeat purchase.
- Repeatable delivery systems can reduce dependence on one-off improvisation; they do not guarantee recurring revenue.
